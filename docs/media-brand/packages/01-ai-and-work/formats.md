## Website introduction — What changes when AI takes over tasks?

[Read the released article and its sources](https://independentobserver.org/research/the-last-human-workforce/#article-sources).

Make a list of the activities inside a job and a change that sounded abstract becomes easier to test. A customer service worker might draft fewer replies but check more suggested ones. If the review includes difficult cases, a measure of writing speed misses some of the new work. The task framework in the article helps separate the transfer of a task from the appearance of new responsibilities. A [field study of customer support agents](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044) gives a concrete example of assistance, with benefits that varied across workers. It does not predict employment elsewhere. The practical question is how training, discretion, quality and pay change alongside productivity. A short version is available on the source page.

## Short 1 — One title, two jobs (30–40 seconds)

[Source for caption](https://independentobserver.org/research/the-last-human-workforce/). Visual: same employee badge, two task strips marked “illustration”.

“A job title can stay the same while the job changes underneath it. Imagine a support agent who used to write every answer from scratch. A tool now suggests the reply, and the agent spends more time checking the exceptions. To measure that shift, list the tasks, who does them, and who can stop a wrong answer. The Independent Observer article in the caption explains why that list tells you more than the title alone.”

## Short 2 — What did 15 percent measure? (30–40 seconds)

[Source for caption](https://independentobserver.org/research/the-last-human-workforce/#workplace-evidence). Visual: final QJE source card, “issues resolved per hour” alongside the number.

“A study of 5,172 customer support agents found that access to an AI assistant raised issues resolved per hour by 15 percent on average. The gains differed across workers, and the study measured one workplace setting. Before repeating a productivity number, ask what was counted and who was studied. You can find the final journal reference and the limits of the result in the linked article.”

## X thread

[Source publication](https://independentobserver.org/research/the-last-human-workforce/).

1. What changes when AI arrives at work? Start with a list of activities inside the job. Drafting, verifying and escalating an unusual case can change in different ways.
2. Automation can move work from people to tools. New responsibilities can also appear. The evidence to look for is where each task went and who can do the new work.
3. One study of 5,172 customer support agents found 15% more issues resolved per hour on average with AI assistance. Effects varied. That measure is specific to the study setting.
4. For a pilot, pair speed with quality, training time and worker discretion. Read the full analysis and the final journal citation: https://independentobserver.org/research/the-last-human-workforce/

## LinkedIn post

A useful question to ask before rolling out an AI tool: what happens to the work that still needs human judgment?

List the activities that move to software, the activities that stay with staff, and the new work the system creates. A customer support study found higher average throughput with AI assistance, with benefits that varied across workers. A rollout still needs its own measures of quality, training and discretion. If exceptions get harder to handle, a faster average may conceal the problem.

[The Last Human Workforce](https://independentobserver.org/research/the-last-human-workforce/) explains the task framework, the study and what it cannot tell us about future employment.

## The Observer Brief

**Subject:** What changes inside a job when AI arrives?

**Preview:** One customer support study and a better way to examine a rollout.

A tool can change the work long before a job title changes. This week’s reading follows the tasks: who drafts, who checks, and who can resolve the exceptions. It links a customer support study and explains why an average productivity gain in that setting does not answer a question about employment elsewhere. If your workplace is running a trial, start with the article’s questions about quality, training and discretion.
