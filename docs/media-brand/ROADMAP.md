# 90-day website, editorial credibility and audience growth roadmap

Updated 9 November 2026. This is the implementation record for the owner's eight-phase plan. Day 1 is 9 November 2026; Day 90 is 6 February 2027. Dates are planning windows, not claims that a release, test or publication happened. The [consolidated audit and implementation report](AUDIT.md) records actual evidence; [measurement](MEASUREMENT.md), the existing [four media packages](README.md), [editorial owner decisions](../editorial-owner-decisions.md), and the established source-to-publication workflow retain their roles.

The objective is reader understanding, trust, retention and voluntary subscriptions. Preserve the four-volume archive, canonical URLs, article identities, original arguments, references and publication approvals. Use topics as the reader entrance and volumes as the intellectual archive. Do not rebuild the site, duplicate research or create another planning system.

## Reader-session protocol and evidence

The publisher recruits consenting readers unfamiliar with the site; this task does not authorize contacting anyone. Early gate: five readers by Day 14. Later rounds: at least five readers per round, ideally new participants, on both desktop and mobile with accessibility needs represented where feasible. Keep participant identifiers, contact details and detailed notes in the private editorial hub, not the public repository. Publish only consented, anonymized aggregates.

Show the homepage for ten seconds, then ask for the publication's purpose in the reader's own words. Ask the reader to find an article matching their interests; identify its principal argument; locate supporting sources and limitations; find another related investigation; and locate the free subscription option. Do not coach. Record unaided success separately from success after help, time to first relevant released article, wrong turns, status misunderstandings, accessibility barriers and reasons not to subscribe. Use a session row with date, tested commit/URL, device/text setting, anonymous participant code, six task outcomes, failure observation and proposed repair. Do not record a completed signup unless the provider confirms it.

The Phase 1 gate requires 4/5 unaided purpose-and-article successes. Later rounds prioritize repeated failures and verify the repair on the tested revision. These sessions cannot establish factual accuracy, academic validation or conversion lift.

## Weekly review and decisions

Use the existing [weekly report template](templates/weekly-report.md). Record work actually released, reader understanding, supported aggregate outcomes, source/rights issues and labour time. Compare equivalent windows and state confounders. Fix the most common reader failure first. Stop extra media cuts if they displace source review. Keep commercial or membership decisions out of this implementation until audience evidence and explicit authorization support them.

Outstanding gates: owner review/merge/release; five-reader first-visitor test; two later testing rounds; Search Console access and 28-day baseline; authorized Substack totals; privacy/provider/consent decision before any new collection; source/rights/channel approval and capacity evaluation for four existing packages. A plan or green CI is not evidence that these gates passed.
