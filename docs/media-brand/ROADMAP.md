# 90-day development roadmap

Day 1 is the first week the reader experience is live and the publisher begins the pilot. Treat milestones below as planned work. The development branch supplies the site changes and production drafts in advance; recording, posting, audience measurement and any commercial pilot depend on separate decisions.

| Period                   | Work                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Evidence to collect                                                                                                                                                                                       | Decision at the end                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Days 1–30: foundation    | Deploy the reviewed homepage, Start Here, editorial standards and Observer Brief. Keep the archive stable. Walk four released editions through the source-to-media checklist. Ask five consenting first-time readers to identify the site's purpose, find a relevant article, locate one source and explain where signup happens. Begin the weekly reporting routine with data already available. | Production check, release record, session notes without personal details, evidence/rights decisions, and first usable aggregate baseline. Set outcome targets only once a baseline exists.                | Fix the first point of repeated confusion. Decide what measurement can be supported within the privacy commitments. Pick one stable publishing cadence.                            |
| Days 31–60: distribution | Review and rehearse the four packages. Run one topic per week only when approved; release fewer assets if source review or production is the constraint. Use the existing website article as home base. Vary one introduction format at a time: question-led vs worked example. Share canonical links in video descriptions, social captions and newsletters.                                     | Readback log, actual production time, qualitative reader response, aggregate channel figures where available, and paid/unpaid distribution context. Record actual publication dates and exposure windows. | Retain formats that readers can understand and the publisher can sustain. If traffic is too small for a useful comparison, treat the test as qualitative.                          |
| Days 61–90: validation   | Compare four topics and formats using matched windows. Review acquisition, article visits, confirmed subscriptions and feedback; flag missing data. Revise weak covers or openings and link to stronger follow-up reading. Select two formats to maintain.                                                                                                                                        | Previous vs current 28-day totals, denominators, source coverage, revisions and evidence that could support a pilot offer. Keep attribution limits visible.                                               | Decide whether to continue as a free publication, test interest in one carefully scoped partnership or licensing offer, or defer commercial work. No payment launch in this cycle. |

At the weekly review, ask: What did readers choose? What did they understand? What evidence can we see? What can we change sustainably? Track labour time per package and stop creating extra cuts if they displace source review. Never compensate for a weak sample by substituting impressions for readership or claiming attribution from timing alone.
