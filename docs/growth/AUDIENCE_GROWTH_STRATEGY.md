# Independent Observer — audience growth strategy

**Status:** Plan; no claims of achieved traffic uplift. **Baseline:** 2026-11-20 main. **Editorial boundary:** no unpublished manuscripts or unapproved source material published.

## Strategic thesis

Grow repeat readership by moving from broad research architecture to clear, evidence-led question journeys. Do not reduce the archive or assume partisanship is needed for growth. Keep all four volumes and all approved topics. Use two initial acquisition themes: **AI, tasks and work opportunity** and **institutional accountability and democratic processes**. Distinguish academic/working papers from short public explanations.

## Primary reader hypothesis and how to test it

**Pilot segment 1:** Workers, workplace educators and technically curious readers who need to evaluate claims about AI and productivity. Their entry point is `/research/the-last-human-workforce/`; the next read connects to factories/jobs and infrastructure. **Pilot segment 2:** Civically engaged readers investigating decision-making, appeal rights and institutional performance. Their entry point is the released democracy article. These are hypotheses, not established audience demographics.

Interview five voluntary first-time readers per pilot where practical. Without coaching, ask them to identify the thesis, trace one source, state one limitation, locate a second article and find the real subscription destination. Record anonymized counts and specific difficulties; no fabricated testimonials, demographic profiling or forced subscription.

## Acquisition, activation and retention

- **Acquisition:** Verify Search Console indexing and the search queries that already lead to canonical pages; optimize descriptions for real questions and link each topic to related released material. Share source-linked extracts through one or two owner-approved platforms, without invented attribution or automated publishing.
- **Activation:** On homepage and article entry, make first reading and next useful source obvious. Preserve version/citation records, but present a concise explanatory hook early.
- **Conversion:** The Observer Brief remains the actual Substack destination. A click-out is *not* a subscription. Do not promise an editorial frequency not maintained.
- **Retention:** Publish genuinely supported updates to existing canonical investigations, surface corrections and enable return visits via the free brief and local continue-reading tools. Repeat visitors are unknown until a privacy-approved analytics source exists.

## Content package rule

For each author-approved published article, create a draft package consisting of a 100-word summary, a 30–60-second social/video script concept, an accurately sourced graphic concept and two useful related links. Publish no derivative that adds unsupported facts or implies a preview is finished.

## Experiments and decision gates

| Experiment | Evaluation | Stop / revise when |
|---|---|---|
| First-read phone test | Source/limitation task completion and observed confusion | Repeated inability to find article or source |
| Search headline/description improvement | Search Console query/page impressions and CTR in comparable settled windows | No reliable search impression denominator |
| One platform distribution | Documented share posts and verifiable referral aggregates | No sustained meaningful engagement or data |
| Newsletter positioning | Confirmed Substack subscriber delta, not external link clicks | Conversion cannot be authenticated |

## Constraints

No new trackers without owner approval, no personal data in public dashboards, no inflammatory or deceptive editorial framing, no hidden paywall, no undeclared AI authorship, no self-generated unapproved investigative 'facts'. Use the established GitHub PR/CI/Vercel workflow, separate the live release from experiments, and retain existing research citations and review dates.
